Cat food: recall record, label standards, and who makes it

Cat food is sold on words like natural, premium and grain-free. The things you can actually check are narrower: whether the label says the food is complete and balanced, what the public recall record shows, and who actually made it. What your own cat should eat, especially with a health condition, is a question for your vet.

The short answer. Look for "complete and balanced" on the label: FDA says that phrase means the food is meant to be your cat's sole diet, backed by an AAFCO nutrient profile or a feeding trial. Then look the brand up on FDA's recall list, remembering FDA says not every recall is posted there. Recalls differ in kind: a warehouse with rodent and bird droppings that held Purina foods in 2025, low thiamine in canned food from 9Lives in 2018 and Wellness in 2011, Salmonella at the plant that made Kirkland Signature cat food in 2012. A brand with no recall on record has no public problem found, which is less than a guarantee.

What the records show

Where the problem happened matters. On 26 December 2025, Minneapolis distributor Gold Star Distribution recalled every FDA-regulated product held at its facility after FDA found rodent excreta, rodent urine and bird droppings where pet food and other goods were stored. The product list attached to the notice includes Purina Cat Chow, Fancy Feast and several Friskies lines. The fault was in storage, not in the factory, and no illnesses had been reported.

Some recalls are about nutrition. In December 2018, J.M. Smucker recalled two 9Lives Protein Plus canned foods for possible low levels of thiamine (vitamin B1), with no illnesses reported. In 2011, WellPet recalled Wellness canned cat food after testing done with FDA, following a consumer complaint, found low thiamine, according to Food Safety News.

Some are about contamination. In April 2021, J.M. Smucker recalled two lots of Meow Mix Original Choice dry food for potential Salmonella, sold at Walmart stores in eight states, with no reports of illness. In December 2019 it recalled two Special Kitty wet foods over ingredients it said did not meet its quality and safety standards.

One plant, many brand names. In 2012, Diamond Pet Foods recalled food made at its Gaston, South Carolina plant after Salmonella Infantis. The recalled labels included Taste of the Wild, Kirkland Signature dog and cat foods, Canidae and Solid Gold. CDC reported people ill in nine states, five of them hospitalised (eFoodAlert). One manufacturer can make food for rival labels.

Inspection records exist. eFoodAlert's review of 2023 FDA inspections found no deficiencies at Purina's Edmond, Oklahoma plant, which makes Cat Chow and Friskies among other lines, while the Crete, Nebraska canning plant had a conveyor fault that let two products mix before the heat step.

Home-cooked is harder than it looks. UC Davis researchers reported in 2019 that of 94 published homemade cat food recipes they could analyse, none met every National Research Council nutrient recommendation for adult cats. Seven percent of the recipes called for a potentially toxic ingredient such as garlic, onion or leek. They advise working with a board-certified veterinary nutritionist instead.

There is no single best cat food. A long recall history can partly reflect how long and how widely a brand has sold, and a young brand with no recall has less history to judge.

Reading the label in 30 seconds

  1. The nutritional adequacy statement. Look for "complete and balanced" and the life stage: growth and reproduction, or adult maintenance. FDA notes treats and supplements are often not complete and balanced.
  2. Who stands behind it. FDA requires the name and place of the manufacturer or distributor. A "distributed by" line names the seller, not necessarily the plant.
  3. The ingredient list. FDA says ingredients appear in descending order by weight.
  4. The lot number and best-by date. Keep the packaging until the food is finished. FDA says the lot number helps it find the plant and production date if something goes wrong.

If something seems wrong

Call your vet if your cat is unwell. You can report a problem food to FDA through its Safety Reporting Portal, and your cat does not have to be sick: FDA lists a foul smell, an off colour, or a swollen or leaking can as reasons to report.

What we're not telling you

We do not rate nutrition, ingredient quality or taste, and we do not tell you what to feed. "No recall found" means no public record turned up on the date we searched. Our records are US only.

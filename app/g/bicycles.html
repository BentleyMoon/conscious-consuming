Bicycles: price, proper assembly, and who backs the bike

Where you get a bike shapes your first year with it more than the model name does. The same money can buy a bike that a mechanic built and will service, a boxed bike you finish yourself, or a stranger's bike with no history. Our explorer compares those routes, since models change every season and the routes change slowly.

The short answer. The cheapest routes carry the least backing, and the best-backed routes cost more or reach fewer people. A local shop fits and services what it sells. A maker's certified pre-owned program inspects and warrants a used bike. A direct-to-consumer brand can save money, but you finish the assembly. A big-box bike can cost you the savings in fixes. The cheapest bike of all is often the one you already own, tuned up, or one given away free nearby.

The routes, one at a time

A local bike shop. Consumer Reports says "a dealer can customize the fit for you" and recommends test-riding locally before buying. It also warns that many smaller shops won't service bikes sold by big-box stores, so check that your local shop will service a bike you buy online.

A big-box store. Consumer Reports says the salespeople "are seldom experts in bicycles, let alone assembly and repairs," and that you might spend any money you saved trying to get a budget bike to work at all.

A direct-to-consumer brand. Priority Bicycles ships bikes "95% assembled" for a flat $39.99 in the contiguous US, or books assembly at a partner shop for $145 to $220. Its frames carry a five-year warranty for the original owner, but the warranty does not cover return shipping or labor. Canyon offers a 30-day trial with a full refund if the bike comes back like new in its Canyon Bike Guard packaging, and may charge a restocking fee for wear or damage. Consumer Reports advises caution with online brands "that might not be there tomorrow" and names Canyon as a reputable one.

A maker's certified pre-owned program. Trek's Red Barn Refresh puts each used bike through a 151-point inspection, including serial number verification and a check of service bulletins and recalls. It adds a limited lifetime frame warranty for the new owner, a year of free service and a 30-day return guarantee.

A private sale or Marketplace. Facebook's Purchase Protection does not cover local pickups or Messenger transactions, so a cash handover on the street has no company behind it. Theft is the other risk: Bike Index, a nonprofit registry, reports 1,669 stolen bikes recovered through its database in 2022. Look up the serial number before you pay.

Free, earned or shared. Freecycle has more than 5,000 local groups and over 11 million members giving things away free. Bikes Not Bombs runs Earn-A-Bike for ages 12 to 18: participants refurbish a bike and keep it. Citi Bike's annual membership is $239 a year and includes the first 45 minutes of each classic-bike ride, inside its New York and New Jersey service area.

UK: Cycle to Work. The Department for Transport's guidance for employers covers the tax, salary sacrifice and National Insurance rules. It only helps if your employer chooses to offer the scheme.

There is no best route for everyone. A student with tools and time, a commuter who needs the bike working tomorrow, and a parent buying for a child can each choose well and choose differently.

Safety: defer to the people who check

A bike is a vehicle. Before buying used, search the recall databases: the Consumer Product Safety Commission in the US and the Office for Product Safety and Standards in the UK. Have brakes, wheels and steering checked by a qualified mechanic, especially on a secondhand bike or one you assembled yourself. We give no mechanical advice here.

What we're not telling you

We do not rate bike models, frame materials or comfort, and we do not score the environmental cost of making a new bike. Price notes describe general patterns from company pages and Consumer Reports, not any particular bike or shop. Most routes here are US examples; your area may have its own co-ops and schemes.

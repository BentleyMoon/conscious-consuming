B Corporations: the certification record, the score, and who owns the company

A B Corp logo on a package is a claim, and B Lab keeps a public record behind it. Every company here has a page on B Lab's register showing when it certified, which version of the standard it met, and for most, a score split into five areas. This guide shows you how to read that page, what the score leaves out, and why ownership needs its own look.

The short answer. Check the register, not the packaging. B Corp certifies a whole company: it starts with the company's own self-assessment, which B Lab says is then audited by a third party. On the older standard most companies here hold, 80 points qualified, against a median of 50.9 for ordinary businesses that take the assessment. Patagonia's page shows 166.0, Ben & Jerry's 96.0. The score says nothing about the owner: Ben & Jerry's board approved Unilever's takeover offer in 2000, while King Arthur Baking says it is 100% employee-owned. There is no single best B Corp. Decide whether you weigh verified practice, the owner, or the price, and look each one up.

What a B Corp score measures, and what it leaves out

It measures company practice across five areas: governance, workers, community, environment and customers, each shown on older-standard pages. A total can sit on uneven areas: Patagonia's 166.0 includes 79.2 for Community and 4.7 for Customers. If one area matters most to you, read that line.

It does not certify a product. The certification belongs to the legal entity that applied, such as "Ben & Jerry's Homemade, Inc." or "Danone USA PBC". Check that the entity matches the brand you are buying.

New certifications carry no single score. Nespresso Global's page shows Version 2.2 and certification year 0, and explains that companies meet Year 0 requirements to certify, then add more in Years 3 and 5. Its old-system scores, 84.3 in 2022 and 105.4 in 2025, remain in the page data but do not convert. Our certification figure halves each register score to fit a 0 to 100 scale, so read Nespresso's cell with its note.

Complaints have a narrow door. B Lab's public complaints process investigates intentional misrepresentation during certification and breaches of the community's core values. It generally will not take up refunds, shipping delays, or a company skipping a practice certification does not require. Outcomes run from certification upheld to revoked.

Certification travels with the entity that applied, so a certified company can be a wholly owned subsidiary, as Nespresso Global's page shows. The owner is who has the final say when the mission and the profit pull apart.

A two-minute check before you buy

  1. Find the company on B Lab's register and match the certified entity to the brand on the label.
  2. Note the standards version, the certified-since date, and the area you care about.
  3. Look up the owner separately: a filing, a company registry, or the company's own page.

What we are not telling you

We do not audit these companies; we report what B Lab's register and the ownership records say. B Corp certifies a company, not a product's ingredients or materials. We hold prices for only four of the thirteen companies, so cost is mostly not scored yet. Scores change at each recertification.

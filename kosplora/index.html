Twenty-four longer study routes

The reading shelf holds 24 study routes. Each one runs the same five stages, orient, study, practice, make and review, and ends in something another person can look at: a deployed website, a household emergency plan, published OpenStreetMap edits. They ask between 6 and 48 hours, spread over 3 to 10 weeks. Growing food asks only 20 hours and still takes ten weeks, because tomatoes keep their own schedule.

The study routes are older than the route file format and written in a different shape, so the format's checks do not run on them. The same shelf ranks 18 places to learn by your own values file, with the source for each score.

What a route promises

  • It ends. Every station is visible from the start, spaced by the time it actually takes, and the last one is something you make.
  • Some stations are people and places. A conversation, a visit or an hour of practice teaches some things better than a page can, so the page hands you over and waits.
  • Every source shows its author and date, and every route says what would change it. The disagreement station has to quote a real published voice, because a model arguing with itself is the same opinion asked twice.
  • What you write stays yours. Your conditions, marks and notes live in this browser until you export them as files. The route itself is a text file in an open format, so a walker I did not write can read it too.

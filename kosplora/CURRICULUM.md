# Kosplora guided curriculum demonstration

**Research round:** 2026-07-22
**Status:** curated demonstration, not accreditation
**Source of truth:** `kosplora/curriculum.js`

Kosplora now has two complementary layers:

1. **The open university shelves** preserve a broad curator map of channels and useful places on the web.
2. **Guided study routes** turn selected resources into learning sequences with outcomes, prerequisites, time, practice, a made artifact, and delayed review.

The signed 18-resource decision receipt remains unchanged. The curriculum is a skin-layer demonstration and therefore does not silently promote illustrative scoring into release-grade educational endorsement.

## The learning loop

Every route uses the same five-stage shape:

1. **Orient:** build the map and name the question.
2. **Study:** learn the central concepts from structured material.
3. **Practice:** retrieve, solve, test, annotate, or observe.
4. **Make:** produce a durable artifact another person can inspect.
5. **Review:** revisit after delay, seek disconfirming evidence, and revise.

This design follows established guidance on spacing, active retrieval, worked examples, explanatory questions, multiple representations, barrier-aware design, and reflective review. It intentionally refuses to equate watching with mastery.

## Research anchors

- [Learning how to learn](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/education-development/learning-how-learn/) provides a free, activity-based introduction to reflection and learning strategy.
- [Organizing Instruction and Study to Improve Student Learning](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1) provides evidence-rated guidance on spacing, interleaving, worked examples, quizzing, graphics with words, and explanatory questions.
- [Universal Design for Learning Guidelines 3.0](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/) frames accessibility around multiple ways to engage, understand, act, and express learning.
- [UNESCO's Recommendation on Open Educational Resources](https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/recommendation-open-educational-resources-oer) distinguishes open permissions from no-cost access.
- [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) and [WAI Easy Checks](https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/preliminary/) inform the accessibility checks in the web route while making clear that a quick automated scan is not conformance proof.

## Demonstration scope

- 12 guided routes
- 60 route stages
- 92 source-checked resource records
- 9 expanded discovery shelves
- local progress only; no account, telemetry, or server state

### Routes

- **Build a learning system**: 3 weeks, about 2 hours per week; finishes with a one-page learning protocol, 20 retrieval prompts, and a two-week review calendar.
- **Read data without being fooled**: 6 weeks, about 4 hours per week; finishes with a reproducible one-page data story with a chart, source note, uncertainty paragraph, and a list of checks.
- **Write an evidence-based argument**: 5 weeks, about 4 hours per week; finishes with a 1,200-word argument, annotated source log, and revision memo explaining the largest change.
- **Build an accessible website**: 8 weeks, about 6 hours per week; finishes with a deployed single-page website, accessibility checklist, test notes, and known-limitations statement.
- **Investigate a claim on the open web**: 4 weeks, about 3 hours per week; finishes with an evidence dossier with a claim table, source chain, confidence statement, and correction history.
- **Understand climate evidence locally**: 6 weeks, about 4 hours per week; finishes with a local climate briefing with one observed trend, one regional projection, uncertainties, and clearly separated policy choices.
- **Follow a public decision from rule to action**: 5 weeks, about 3 hours per week; finishes with a two-page civic process map for one local or national issue, with primary sources and a concrete participation step.
- **Read a health claim safely**: 5 weeks, about 3 hours per week; finishes with a health-evidence brief with claim type, study design, absolute effects, limitations, conflicts, and questions for a clinician.
- **Build history from primary sources**: 6 weeks, about 4 hours per week; finishes with a mini digital exhibit with five primary sources, captions, provenance, a guiding question, and an interpretation note.
- **Start a language through use**: 8 weeks, about 3 hours per week; finishes with a three-minute recording or live conversation, a personal phrasebook, and 30 spaced prompts drawn from real use.
- **Run a small scientific investigation**: 6 weeks, about 4 hours per week; finishes with a short investigation report with question, model, method, data, uncertainty, and a replication or parameter-change result.
- **Run a family curiosity studio**: 4 weeks, about 2 hours per week; finishes with a family curiosity portfolio with one story response, one investigation, one creative project, and one child-led explanation.

## Resource metadata

Each resource record includes:

- provider and canonical URL;
- format;
- access class;
- license or rights posture;
- account requirement;
- bandwidth posture;
- downloadability;
- a short reason for inclusion;
- source class; and
- the date the provider page was reviewed.

`open` means reuse rights are stated. `free` means no-cost access was available when checked. `mixed` means an account, eligibility rule, paid option, or rights restriction matters. These are not interchangeable.

The checked date is an editorial review date, not a guarantee that a third-party page remains unchanged. A future maintenance pass should add automated link status, redirect history, and category-specific review dates.

## Safety and evidence boundaries

The old advice that treated malware-laden download hunting as educational friction has been removed from the live surface. The replacement guidance prioritizes public libraries, interlibrary loan, openly licensed texts, public-domain collections, repositories, and author-posted manuscripts.

Health and legal routes teach evidence literacy. They do not provide diagnosis, treatment, or legal advice. Learners are prompted to stop online research and seek qualified help when the stakes exceed a learning exercise.

Automated accessibility tools are presented as partial checks, not conformance proof. The web route requires keyboard, zoom, narrow-screen, and human-centered review, and asks the learner to disclose untested areas.

## Editorial principles used in this round

- Prefer primary, official, nonprofit, university, standards, and public-infrastructure sources.
- Distinguish open licensing from free access.
- Prefer a small complete route over an enormous unsequenced shelf.
- Require every route to end in an inspectable artifact.
- Make uncertainty, perspective, jurisdiction, eligibility, and source rights visible.
- Keep resources with access restrictions when they uniquely serve disabled learners, but label the restriction plainly.
- Preserve local control: progress remains in the browser and can be reset per route.

## Maintenance next

This is a credible demonstration, not the end state. The highest-value next research is inter-rater review of resource metadata, disabled-learner testing, non-English route co-design, link and license monitoring, and route outcome testing with real learners.

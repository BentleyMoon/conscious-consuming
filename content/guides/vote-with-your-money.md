# Vote with your money: where to start

Your spending funds whatever made the thing: the wages it paid, the river it used, the carbon it burned, the company behind it. Most of us cast those votes blind. This page is a place to start seeing a little more clearly and choosing a few things on purpose. Not perfection. A few deliberate votes, kept up.

> **The short answer.** You cannot fix the world at the checkout, and you do not have to. Start with the few choices that carry the most money or repeat for years, such as banking, clothing, or a major device. Use sourced facts, stay inside your budget, and choose one or two changes you can keep. Advice that ignores cost excludes the people it claims to help.

---

## Does it actually work?

One purchase is a drop. But drops aggregate, and markets do move when enough people vote the same way. Fair Trade, cage-free eggs, plant-based milk, and fossil-free banking all grew because demand shifted. Your vote is small, real, and it compounds when it's a habit rather than a one-off act of heroism.

Two caveats, so you neither over-rate nor under-rate it:
- **The biggest levers are usually collective.** Policy, organizing, and how institutions invest move more than any basket ever will. Spending deliberately and acting together aren't rivals; do both at whatever scale you can.
- **You are not personally responsible for fixing capitalism at the supermarket.** The goal is pointing your everyday money a little more deliberately, not carrying the planet on your receipts.

## Where your vote is loudest

Not everything matters equally, and treating it as if it does is the fast road to burnout. A rough ranking of leverage:

1. **Where your money sleeps.** Your bank and pension lend and invest your money around the clock, often into fossil fuels. Moving savings to a fossil-free bank can outweigh years of careful grocery choices, and it takes an afternoon.
2. **Big, repeat, or durable purchases.** Clothing, your phone and laptop, energy, transport. Fewer decisions, bigger footprints.
3. **Daily staples you buy on repeat.** Coffee, chocolate, cleaning, paper goods. Small each time, but constant.
4. **The fiddly small stuff.** Which exact toothpaste? Genuinely the least important. Don't sweat it.

## Claims to verify

- **Greenwashing.** "Natural," "eco," "conscious," and "clean" are mostly unregulated mood words. Trust a certification or a sourced fact, not the front of the box.
- **The perfection trap.** Don't let the perfect choice block the better one. A bank switch you actually make beats a flawless plan you never do.
- **The guilt trap.** This is about agency, not shame. If a guide makes you feel small, it's a bad guide.
- **The privilege blind spot.** Sometimes the cheapest option is the only option, and that is completely fine. Cost is one of the values you can weigh here for exactly that reason; a practice that only works on a comfortable income isn't much of a practice.

## How to actually start

Pick one thing this month and make it stick:

- Move your savings, or just opt your pension into its ethical fund. Biggest effect, smallest effort.
- Buy your next clothes secondhand, or from a brand that publishes its suppliers.
- Or just start noticing: read one fact past the marketing on something you already buy.

Then, when a real decision comes up, use this tool to compare actual options by your values. The weighting is yours, the facts carry their sources, and nothing here is sponsored.

## A practical default

You don't have to care about everything. Choose what is genuinely yours to care about, aim your money there, and let the rest go without guilt. A few deliberate votes, kept up over years, add up to something real.

# B Corporations: the certification record, the score, and who owns the company

A B Corp logo on a package is a claim, and B Lab keeps a public record behind it. Every company here has a page on B Lab's register showing when it certified, which version of the standard it met, and for most, a score split into five areas. This guide shows you how to read that page, what the score leaves out, and why ownership needs its own look.

> **The short answer.** Check the register, not the packaging. B Corp certifies a whole company: it starts with the company's own self-assessment, which B Lab says is then [audited by a third party](https://www.bcorporation.net/en-us/certification/). On the older standard most companies here hold, 80 points qualified, against a median of 50.9 for ordinary businesses that take the assessment. Patagonia's page shows 166.0, Ben & Jerry's 96.0. The score says nothing about the owner: Ben & Jerry's board approved Unilever's takeover offer in 2000, while King Arthur Baking says it is 100% employee-owned. There is no single best B Corp. Decide whether you weigh verified practice, the owner, or the price, and look each one up.

| Area scores | Where the points came from | Patagonia: Community 79.2, Environment 40.4, Workers 23.7, Governance 17.8, Customers 4.7 |
| Previous scores | The record over time | Patagonia: 107.3 in 2011, 151.4 in 2020, 166.0 now |

## What a B Corp score measures, and what it leaves out

**It measures company practice across five areas:** governance, workers, community, environment and customers, each shown on older-standard pages. A total can sit on uneven areas: Patagonia's 166.0 includes 79.2 for Community and 4.7 for Customers. If one area matters most to you, read that line.

**It does not certify a product.** The certification belongs to the legal entity that applied, such as "Ben & Jerry's Homemade, Inc." or "Danone USA PBC". Check that the entity matches the brand you are buying.

**New certifications carry no single score.** Nespresso Global's page shows Version 2.2 and certification year 0, and explains that companies meet Year 0 requirements to certify, then add more in Years 3 and 5. Its old-system scores, 84.3 in 2022 and 105.4 in 2025, remain in the page data but do not convert. Our certification figure halves each register score to fit a 0 to 100 scale, so read Nespresso's cell with its note.

**Complaints have a narrow door.** B Lab's [public complaints process](https://www.bcorporation.net/en-us/standards/complaints/) investigates intentional misrepresentation during certification and breaches of the community's core values. It generally will not take up refunds, shipping delays, or a company skipping a practice certification does not require. Outcomes run from certification upheld to revoked.

## Ownership is a separate question

| Company | Register score | Who owns it, per the record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Patagonia | 166.0 | A purpose trust holds the voting stock (2%); the nonprofit Holdfast Collective holds the rest (98%) |
| King Arthur Baking | 116.4 | "100% employee-owned", per its own site |
| Klean Kanteen | 110.9 | "a family-owned B Corp", per its register description |
| Tony's Chocolonely | 125.0 | After outside investment, 51% of voting rights stayed with "our very own Henk Jan"; 15% of shares with Team Tony's |
| Divine Chocolate | 114.9 | Companies House lists Papa Paa GmbH with 75% or more since 29 May 2020, the day the Kuapa Kokoo farmers' union stopped being listed with significant control |
| Seventh Generation | 100.6 | Its site serves "products and services of Unilever United States" |
| Ben & Jerry's | 96.0 | Board approved Unilever's offer in April 2000, valued at $326 million |
| Nespresso Global | none (Version 2.2) | "Wholly-owned subsidiary", on its register page |

Certification travels with the entity that applied, so a certified company can be a wholly owned subsidiary, as Nespresso Global's page shows. The owner is who has the final say when the mission and the profit pull apart.

## If you care most about...

| Your priority | Start with | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verified practice across the company | The register page | It is B Lab's record, not the brand's summary |
| Who keeps the profit and the final say | Steward-, employee- or family-owned companies | A score says nothing about the owner |
| One issue, such as climate or workers | The area score, not the total | Totals hide uneven areas |
| Spending less | Keeping what you have, or buying it used | See [secondhand and resale](#guide/secondhand-and-resale) |

## A two-minute check before you buy

1. Find the company on B Lab's register and match the certified entity to the brand on the label.
2. Note the standards version, the certified-since date, and the area you care about.
3. Look up the owner separately: a filing, a company registry, or the company's own page.

## What we are not telling you

We do not audit these companies; we report what B Lab's register and the ownership records say. B Corp certifies a company, not a product's ingredients or materials. We hold prices for only four of the thirteen companies, so cost is mostly not scored yet. Scores change at each recertification.

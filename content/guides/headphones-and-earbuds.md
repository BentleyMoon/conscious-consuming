# Headphones and earbuds: repairability, battery life, and what they collect

Headphones wear out in predictable places. iFixit names cables and earpads as the usual trouble on over-ear models, and every wireless pair adds a battery, which some makers let you replace and others glue in. How a pair handles those parts has a lot to do with how long it lasts.

> **The short answer.** The pair you already own, with a new cable, earpads or battery, is often the best next pair. When you do buy, check two things before sound: can the battery be replaced, and does the maker sell parts? Wired pairs like the ones below have no battery to wear out and no companion app. There is no single best pair, but iFixit's repair scores for the wireless models here run from 0 to 10, so the choice matters.

## Repair first

iFixit's [headphone repair hub](https://www.ifixit.com/Device/Headphone) says the most common problems with over-ear headphones come from the cables and earpads. It sells batteries, earpads and cables, and advises: "look for headphones with replaceable cables and earpads." A worn earpad or frayed cable is usually a small part, not a reason to replace the pair.

## Long battery life is not the same as a long life

A big number on the box describes a new battery. The Soundcore Q20i lists [60 hours with noise cancelling off](https://www.soundcore.com/eu/products/q20i-a3004g11) for €52.99, yet scores 2 for repair. The Fairbuds XL list [30 hours, or 26 with noise cancelling](https://www.fairphone.com/fairbuds-xl), for €249, with a battery you swap yourself and three years of warranty. The Fairbuds earbuds cost [€99](https://www.fairphone.com/fairbuds) and have replaceable batteries in each bud and the case.

Warranty can bridge the gap. Bose's [QuietComfort Ultra Headphones](https://www.bose.com/p/headphones/bose-quietcomfort-ultra-headphones-2nd-gen/QCUH2-HEADPHONEARN.html) cost $449 with a one-year warranty covering battery failure, and a $59.95 plan extends that cover for two more years.

Wired pairs skip the problem. Sennheiser's [HD 600](https://us.sennheiser-hearing.com/products/hd-600) costs $499.95, with a detachable cable, $54.95 replacement earpads and a two-year warranty. Shure's [SE215](https://www.shure.com/en-US/products/earphones/se215) in-ear monitors use a detachable cable that Shure sells separately. Apple's wired [EarPods with USB-C](https://www.target.com/p/apple-earpods-usb-c/-/A-89827259) are $19.99 at Target.

## What the device and its app collect

Many wireless models pair with a companion app, and the app has its own privacy policy. Bose's [policy](https://www.bose.com/legal/privacy-policy) lists sensor data such as head orientation and movement, fit, volume levels, and "noise levels and audio frequencies of sounds around you." Read the policy for the app you would install, and skip the app if the headphones work without it.

## Buying secondhand, and the charger you already have

Back Market's [promise](https://www.backmarket.com/en-us/quality) includes a one-year warranty and free 30-day returns, though its inspection checklist names phones, tablets, smartwatches, laptops and consoles, not headphones. Check the exact model's repair score before you buy.

In the EU, the [common charger rules](https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/eu-common-charger-rules-power-all-your-devices-single-charger-2024-12-28_en) have covered headphones, headsets and earbuds since 28 December 2024, so rechargeable models sold there charge over USB-C, and you can buy new devices without a charger. A USB-C cable you already own can charge them.

## If you care most about...

There is no single best pair. Match the choice to what you weigh most.

| If you care most about | Look at | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keeping one pair for years | Wired, or a wireless model scoring high with iFixit | Nothing sealed that wears out |
| Lowest price | Wired earbuds, or repairing what you have | The cheapest pair in our data is $19.99 |
| Noise cancelling that lasts | An over-ear model with a screwed or swappable battery | The battery is the part that ages |
| Privacy | Wired headphones | No radio, no app, no account |
| Less new manufacturing | Repair, or a refurbished pair with a return window | Uses what already exists |

## What we are not telling you

We do not score sound. It is a matter of taste, and we have no independent way to test it. Some models have no score for some measures because the maker's page did not state a figure. Prices were checked in September 2026 and change often. If you have concerns about your hearing, or want to use earbuds as a hearing aid, talk to an audiologist or your doctor; we do not assess hearing features.

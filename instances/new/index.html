What the builder does, and what it cannot

What's happening. The form on the left writes a facts file, lens.js, as you type. The panel on the right loads that file into the shared engine and ranks your options by the weights on its sliders. A score only counts once its source box has at least three characters in it.

Why it matters. Most of the work in a values app is finding the facts. This lets a teacher, a co-op buyer or a tenants' group find out whether their decision works as a ranking before anyone writes software.

Devil's advocate. The lock only checks that a source box has something in it. It cannot tell a certification register from "my uncle said", which is also more than three characters. The scores are yours, and nothing here checks them. The downloaded page also fetches the engine from valuescommons.org, so it needs the internet the first time, or a copy of engine.js beside it.

Rabbit hole. Read Where to Message's lens.js to see a finished one, then the build guide for everything past a first draft.
